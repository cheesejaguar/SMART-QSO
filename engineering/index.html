Engineering: the CDR 2026 design review

On 2026-10-27 the project finished a design review against the NASA NPR 7123.1D Critical Design Review criteria, tailored for a Class D CubeSat. This page shows what it found, what it decided, and what it left for hardware and for the principal investigator (PI). Report: SQSO-REV-CDR-002 version B

What the review is, and is not

In the report's own words (§1.2):

  • It is an engineering assessment against the CDR entrance and success criteria of NPR 7123.1D Appendix G (Table G-7), tailored for a Class D CubeSat, carried out by AI reviewers (Claude) working on the repository: seven discipline reviewers, five designers and a chair. Every finding is a Review Item Discrepancy (RID) with a recorded disposition and evidence.
  • It is not an independent review board. There are no named human reviewers and no signatures. The funding audit flagged the earlier review records for exactly this (FINDINGS_REGISTER PI-03, PI-04); this record does not repair that. The project's independent PDR and CDR are scheduled (mission/SCHEDULE.md); this review is their input.
  • The programmatics reviewer found that the project is at pre-PDR maturity by the NPR 7123.1D criteria (no independent SRR or PDR, no engineering model; docs/project/CDR_CRITERIA_MATRIX.md). The review therefore also did the design work a CDR presumes: schematic capture of the boards with selected parts, a mechanical CAD model and a finite-element model (§5).
  • An action that needs hardware cannot close by analysis. Each one is a verification item with a test, a pass criterion and a date. An action that needs an outside party (a contract, an agreement, a filing) is a decision point with an owner and a date. Neither is counted as an open review action; both are tracked in the schedule and the verification matrix.

Verdict and maturity

No review action is open. Every one of the 412 findings carries a final disposition. "Closed" means the evidence is recorded in the repository; where only hardware or an outside party can supply it, the finding is closed as a dated verification item or decision point, not as a result. SQSO-REV-CDR-002 §7.1

The report's maturity statement (§7.2):

Judged honestly against Table G-7, SMART-QSO is at the level of a strong PDR with CDR-depth analysis, not past a CDR:

  • No independent board has reviewed it. This review is an engineering assessment by AI reviewers (§1.2); the independent PDR is planned for 2027-01-12 and the independent CDR for 2027-09-28.
  • No PCB layout and no engineering model exist. The board designs are schematics with selected parts, ERC-clean, with their calculations.
  • Every figure is predicted, none measured: mass, power, thermal, structural, RF and attitude. The firmware has not run on hardware and its pin map is provisional.
  • Several values rest on vendor statements not yet in hand, each a dated action (SQSO-MFG-003 §12):
    • the ISIS AntS cold limits and its temperature command;
    • the CR0002 rod rating;
    • the RD01MUS3 minimum temperature;
    • the CC1200 TX-OFF leakage;
    • the fuse I²t data.

What it gives the independent PDR is a design with no known open defect, the evidence for every claim, and the list of what only hardware can show.

Risks the review accepts

  • AntS temperature gate. The gate relies on the ISIS temperature command (assumed until AV-V7 confirms it). Off the nominal path, the spacecraft can be silent for about 26 hours. The cover is a blind forced deployment from the ground (C-18, C-19).
  • Pointing. Pointing meets its power-derived requirement (C-23) only up to a 7 mA·m² residual dipole (C-24). There is no margin at that limit, so the flight unit's dipole test is a hard gate.
  • Smart Beacon experiment. Its 0.1 Wh go/no-go is met in simulation under assumed inputs (0.065 Wh). Only VI-02 on the EM decides it. The fixed-template beacon is the flight baseline regardless (D5).
  • Fuses below rating. The end-of-life fuse F1 and the backup overcharge fuse F3 are below the −40 °C part rule. They are covered by placement, by the firing rules and by flight-lot qualification (V-EPS-8), not by their ratings.
  • Bus parts without radiation data. Most bus parts have no radiation data. They are covered by lot testing and screening before the CDR (RAD-V3, RAD-V4); a part that fails is changed before the FM layout.
